# The Operational Primitives

*Interaction Dynamics · 2026*

---

## Abstract

Three structural primitives — Influence, Differentiation, Constraint — constitute the minimal operational description of structure formation in any dynamic system. They are not proposed as a convenient analytical vocabulary. They are argued to be the irreducible conditions that any system must instantiate to produce stable patterns from interaction. This paper presents the primitive triad, its internal architecture, the reduction to a single conceptual root, an open theoretical consequence about structural inevitability, the composite layer that sits above the working primitives, and cross-field observations of where the triad already lives.

---

## 1. The Claim

Remove any one of the three operational primitives and the description of structure formation fails.

Without Influence, nothing propagates. Without Differentiation, no pattern can exist. Without Constraint, interaction produces noise rather than structure.

Together they yield the minimal operational statement:

> **interaction = influence operating under constraint producing differentiation**

This is not a definition of interaction. It is a description of what interaction must do to produce anything stable. The three primitives name three conditions, not three components.

---

## 2. The Primitives

| Primitive | Role |
|---|---|
| **Influence (I)** | Forces acting upon system state from internal or external sources. The input dimension. Without it, nothing changes. |
| **Differentiation (D)** | Detection of structural differences within the state field. The discriminative function. Without it, no distinction exists and no pattern can form. |
| **Constraint (C)** | Limits governing possible state transitions. The pressure that forces pattern stabilization. Without it, change produces no structure. |

The interaction engine runs as a state recursion:

> S(t+1) = C( D( I( S(t) ) ) )

Each iteration passes the state through all three primitives in sequence. What survives repeated iteration under constraint is what becomes structural. This recursion is not a model of interaction — it is the minimal description of how structure forms through interaction at all.

---

## 3. The Three-Layer Hierarchy

The primitives sit within a three-layer structure. Confusing the layers produces either false reductions or false complexity.

| Layer | Content | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Layer 1 — Conceptual root** | Differentiation alone. The existence of A ≠ B. | Philosophical foundation and consistency anchor. Not a working tool. |
| **Layer 2 — Operational primitives** | Influence · Differentiation · Constraint | The working layer. Minimal, irreducible, expressive enough to describe real systems. |
| **Layer 3 — Composite primitives** | Space · Time · Structure · Coherence · Memory · ... | Stable configurations of the operational primitives. Not additional fundamentals — recurring patterns observed at scale. |

The reduction from Layer 2 to Layer 1 is always available. It is not the working level.

---

## 4. The Conceptual Root

All three operational primitives reduce to Differentiation.

Influence is differentiation affecting differentiation — propagation of distinction through the field. Constraint is differentiation limiting how differentiation propagates — boundaries on the propagation of distinction. Differentiation is the root itself: the existence of difference.

Under this reduction, interaction becomes recursive differentiation:

> Δ(t+1) = f( Δ(t), constraints )
>
> interaction = differentiation propagating under constrained pathways producing new differentiations

This reduction is philosophically valid and serves as a consistency anchor — proof that the framework is internally unified under a single concept.

It is operationally sterile. Reducing every description to a single primitive produces statements that are technically correct and unworkable for reasoning. Three primitives give three independent levers. One primitive gives nothing to grip.

Both are true at different levels. The reduction confirms internal unity. The working layer maintains operational traction. Neither displaces the other.

---

## 5. The Inevitability Consequence

If Differentiation is the conceptual root and Influence and Constraint are both modes Differentiation takes — propagation and limitation — then the three operational primitives may be not just minimal but structurally inevitable.

The argument: any dynamic system capable of producing stable patterns must instantiate all three. Distinctions must exist. They must affect each other. They must be bounded. A system without Influence produces no change. A system without Differentiation produces no pattern. A system without Constraint produces noise. There is no fourth condition to add and no condition to remove.

If this holds, the primitive triad is not a useful analytical choice — it is what dynamic systems are made of, whether or not an observer names it.

This consequence is not yet formally proven within the framework. It is an open theoretical claim worth investigating. If it holds, the framework's claim to universality is substantially strengthened: the primitives would be not just sufficient but necessary for any system in which stable structure appears.

---

## 6. Composite Primitives

The operational primitives do not produce constructs directly. They produce structure. Stable configurations of the primitives that recur across complex systems constitute composite primitives — constructs that appear reliably enough across sufficiently complex systems to warrant treatment as independent reasoning units.

| Composite | As primitive configuration |
|---|---|
| **Time** | Ordered differentiation: Δ₁ → Δ₂ → Δ₃. Requires influence (change), constraint (ordering), differentiation (distinguishable states). |
| **Space** | Structured differentiation across relations. Positions are distinguishable with limits on propagation. |
| **Coherence** | Bounded variance in differentiation across response channels. The primitives in a stable coupled configuration. |
| **Memory** | Constraint carrying information about past differentiation forward. Prior states influencing current constraint structure. |

These are not exhaustive. They are examples of the level — composites that any sufficiently complex dynamic system will instantiate, each derivable from the primitive triad without additional assumptions.

## 7. Cross-Field Observations

The primitive triad does not appear for the first time in this framework. It appears wherever stable patterns form from interaction — named differently by each field, arrived at independently, never unified into a single description.

These are observations, not proofs. The fields are not witnesses to the framework's claims. They are instances of the same structure operating in different substrates.

---

**Physics**

Force propagates through a field, acting on states that were previously undisturbed — Influence. The field is not uniform; gradients exist, symmetries break, phases distinguish themselves — Differentiation. Conservation laws, boundary conditions, and thermodynamic limits determine which state transitions are possible — Constraint. Structure formation in physics — from particle interaction to phase transitions to cosmological large-scale structure — runs on this triad without naming it as such.

**Biology**

Morphogenesis is the clearest case. Morphogen gradients propagate through tissue — Influence. Concentration differences cross thresholds, triggering distinct developmental programs — Differentiation. Positional information and gene regulatory networks bound which programs activate where — Constraint. The body plan emerges not from a blueprint but from the triad operating recursively across developmental time.

**Information Theory**

Shannon's framework requires a source, a signal that varies, and a channel with capacity limits. A source with no variation transmits nothing — no Differentiation, no information. A channel with no limits is not a channel — no Constraint, no structure. Influence carries the signal across the channel. Remove any one condition and information in Shannon's sense ceases to exist.

**Dynamical Systems**

Attractors are where the triad stabilizes. The system is driven by influence, trajectories differentiate across the state space, and constraint — through the geometry of the space — determines which trajectories converge and where. The attractor literature is a detailed formal treatment of what happens when all three primitives operate together over time. It describes the composite layer without naming what produces it.

**Cognitive Science**

Perception requires that something acts on the sensory system — Influence. The system must detect differences in what acts on it — Differentiation. Attention, prior expectation, and neural architecture determine what differences register and how — Constraint. Pattern recognition, learning, and concept formation each instantiate the triad at their respective levels.

---

These fields did not converge by communication. Physics did not borrow from morphogenesis. Information theory did not borrow from dynamical systems. Each arrived at the same structural requirements from different starting conditions and named them in domain-specific vocabulary.

What the primitive layer makes possible is not a new discovery in any of these fields. It is legibility across them. A result about constraint in thermodynamics and a result about constraint in developmental biology are, at the primitive level, about the same thing operating in different substrates. Currently those fields do not read each other at that level because the shared vocabulary does not exist. A unified primitive description provides it — not by replacing the domain-specific frameworks, which remain the appropriate working level within each field, but by sitting below them as a common substrate.

A less obvious implication concerns fields in formation. When a domain is developing and its own proper vocabulary has not yet stabilized, the primitive layer offers a temporary working scaffold. Reasoning in {I, D, C} terms — identifying what is propagating, what is being distinguished, what is bounding the transitions — allows structural analysis to proceed before field-specific constructs exist. When those constructs do arrive, they arrive already located in the primitive architecture. What changes across configurations is which elements play which roles. The primitives remain stable underneath.

The diagnostic implication follows directly: when a system fails to produce stable patterns, the primitive layer identifies where the failure is. Absent influence, nothing propagates. Absent differentiation, nothing is distinguished. Absent constraint, nothing stabilizes. This holds regardless of domain. The failure mode is the same structure, the same diagnosis, a different substrate.

Whether this unified layer is worth the additional abstraction is a question this paper does not answer. The structure is offered as an observation about where {I, D, C} already lives, not as a prescription for how any field should operate. If the primitive level illuminates something that was stuck, it earns its place. If it complicates without return, it doesn't. That determination belongs to whoever is working the problem.

---

## 8. Identity

Identity is a composite primitive. It enters at Layer 3 and inherits the full operational architecture. Its treatment — anchor formation, reconstruction, field topology, measurement — requires its own framework. That framework is developed separately.

What this paper establishes is the substrate that framework builds on.
