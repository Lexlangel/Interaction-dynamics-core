# Framework Validation Protocol

*Interaction Dynamics · 2026*

---

## Purpose

This protocol defines the conditions I imposed on myself before presenting this framework.

It is not claimed to be complete, universal, or final.

It is a working attempt to introduce structural discipline into theory-building in a context where AI is rapidly accelerating the production of new frameworks.

---

## Context

We are entering a phase where:

- The number of proposed theories is increasing rapidly
- The cost of generating convincing arguments is decreasing
- The distinction between *plausible* and *structurally sound* is becoming harder to track

Because of this, evaluation methods must evolve alongside generation methods.

This protocol is one attempt to address that.

---

## Position

I do not claim that:

- This framework is correct
- All dependencies are fully resolved
- All implications have been explored

I do claim that:

> These are the conditions I required the framework to satisfy before presenting it.

---

## Core Validation Conditions

A framework is considered **structurally acceptable (provisionally)** if it satisfies the following:

---

### 1. Dependency Clarity

All core concepts must:

- Be explicitly defined
- Have their dependencies mapped
- Avoid implicit equivalence (e.g., "X is just Y") unless justified

*Goal: prevent hidden assumptions.*

---

### 2. Circularity Exposure

All dependency chains must be examined for loops.

If circular dependencies exist, they must be:

- Explicitly acknowledged
- Or structurally resolved

*Goal: avoid self-supporting definitions.*

---

### 3. Persistence Conditions

Each concept must be tested under interruption:

- Does it persist without memory?
- Without continuity of input?
- Without awareness?

*Goal: distinguish between dependent and independent structures.*

---

### 4. Minimal Mechanism Identification

The framework must attempt to identify:

> The smallest set of primitives required for the phenomenon.

All additional components should be tested for necessity, redundancy, and substitutability.

*Goal: avoid overconstructed explanations.*

---

### 5. Discriminator Requirement

The framework must produce at least one observable that:

- Differentiates it from a null or alternative model
- Can be measured or simulated

*Goal: separate signal from artifact.*

---

### 6. Adversarial Testing

The mechanism must be stress-tested by:

- Inversion (e.g., reversing directionality)
- Noise injection
- Component removal

*Goal: identify load-bearing vs incidental elements.*

---

### 7. Scope Declaration

The framework must explicitly state:

- What it explains
- What it does not explain
- What would be required to extend it

*Goal: prevent overreach.*

---

### 8. Prediction Requirement

The framework should produce at least one of:

- Structural predictions
- Behavioral predictions
- Implementation or architectural implications

*Goal: ensure forward relevance.*

---

### 9. Cross-Domain Mapping *(optional but strong)*

If possible, the framework should demonstrate transferability across substrates.

*Goal: test generality vs artifact.*

---

## Interpretation

This protocol does not prove correctness.

It does:

- Expose structural weaknesses
- Clarify assumptions
- Make comparisons possible

A framework that satisfies these conditions is not *true* — it is **structurally accountable**.

## Quick Diagnostic

| # | Condition | Question | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dependency clarity | Are all core concepts defined with stated relationships? | Yes, explicit |
| 2 | No circularity | Does any concept depend on itself through a chain? | No loops |
| 3 | Persistence | What survives interruption? What breaks? | Both identified |
| 4 | Minimal mechanism | What is the smallest sufficient primitive set? | Stated and tested |
| 5 | Discriminator | Is there an observable separating signal from null? | Yes, measurable |
| 6 | Adversarial | What breaks under inversion, noise, removal? | Tested, documented |
| 7 | Scope | Are limits of the claim explicitly stated? | Yes, before extension |
| 8 | Predictions | Does the framework generate testable predictions? | At least one class |
| 9 | Transfer | Does the mechanism hold across substrates? | Tested or flagged open |

## Limitations

This protocol is:

- Incomplete
- Potentially biased by its own assumptions
- Not yet formally standardized

It is expected that different fields will adapt stricter or looser variants, and that better protocols will emerge over time.

---

## Invitation

This is not intended as a fixed standard.

It is an open proposal.

If this direction is useful, the next step is not adoption but:

> refinement, critique, and expansion into more robust validation systems.

---

## Closing

I do not claim to have followed this protocol perfectly.

I do claim that:

> This is the level of rigor I attempted to hold before presenting the work.

In an environment where ideas can be generated instantly, constraints like these may become as important as the ideas themselves.
